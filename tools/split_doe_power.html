What is power? If a true yield difference of size Δ exists between the control and trial arms, 80% power means the experiment has an 80% chance of detecting it (rejecting the null at p < 0.05) — and a 20% chance of a false negative. At 90% power the false-negative risk drops to 10%, but more wafers are needed.

Rule of thumb: a 1:1 split needs roughly 16 × (σ/Δ)² wafers per arm — halving the detectable delta quadruples the wafer count.

Assumptions: wafer yields are roughly normal and independent within the lot with common σ; the split shares one lot, so lot-to-lot variation cancels. Splits spread across whole lots need a different (larger) calculation.
