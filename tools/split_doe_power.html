Split DOE wafer count estimator

How many wafers does a control-vs-trial yield split need before a real difference stands out from normal wafer-to-wafer spread? Assumes the split runs within one lot and compares mean yield per wafer with a two-sided two-sample t-test at α = 0.05.

Reading the table: each row is a true control-vs-trial yield difference; the cells give the wafer counts needed for an experiment to detect it (i.e. reach p < 0.05) with 80% or 90% probability. Rows marked > lot need more wafers than the lot holds. Power is computed exactly from the noncentral t-distribution (matches statsmodels), so small counts are not flattered.

Rule of thumb: at 80% power an equal split needs roughly 16 × (σ/Δ)² wafers per arm — halving the detectable delta quadruples the wafer count.

Assumptions: wafer yields are roughly normal and independent within the lot with common σ; the split shares one lot, so lot-to-lot variation cancels. Splits spread across whole lots need a different (larger) calculation.
